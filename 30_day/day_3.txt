📘 MAIN LESSON CONTENT
PART 1: BASIC CONVERSATION SKILLS
🎯 Learning Focus: Answering Simple Questions Naturally
Common Mistake:
Many learners memorize only one way to answer questions. For example:
"How are you?" → "I'm fine, thank you."
This sounds robotic and unnatural.
Better Approach:
Native speakers vary their responses based on how they feel. Here are natural alternatives:
Question: "How are you today?"
Natural Responses:
"I'm doing well, thank you."
"I'm great! How about you?"
"I'm okay, a bit tired."
"I'm good, thanks for asking."
"Not bad! And you?"
️ PRACTICE DIALOGUE 1: FIRST MEETING
Situation: You meet someone for the first time.
Person A: Hi! How are you today?
You: I'm doing well, thank you. How about you?
Person A: I'm great! What's your name?
You: My name is [Your Name]. What's yours?
Person A: I'm Alex. Where are you from?
You: I'm from [Your Country]. And you?
Person A: I'm from here. So, what do you do?
You: I'm a student. / I work in [field]. How about you?
Person A: I'm a teacher. Nice to meet you!
You: Nice to meet you too!
📌 KEY LEARNING POINTS
✅ "I'm from" vs. "I come from"
Both are correct, but "I'm from" is more common in everyday speech.
✅ "What do you do?" = What is your job/study?
This is a very common question in English. Practice your answer!
✅ Adding "How about you?" shows interest
It makes the conversation feel natural and friendly.
PART 2: EXTENDED CONVERSATION PRACTICE
🎯 Learning Focus: Speaking in Longer Sentences
The Challenge:
Beginners often speak in very short sentences:
"I study."
"I busy."
"I go friend house."
The Goal:
Speak in complete, natural sentences:
"I've been studying English every day."
"I've been busy with my studies."
"I'm going to visit my friend this weekend."
️ PRACTICE DIALOGUE 2: CATCHING UP WITH A FRIEND
Situation: You haven't seen your friend in a while.
Friend: Hey! Long time no see! How have you been?
You: Hi! Yes, it's been a long time. I've been busy with my studies.
Friend: What have you been studying lately?
You: I've been studying English every day. I watch videos and practice speaking.
Friend: That's great! What's the hardest part for you?
You: The hardest part is speaking. I understand when people talk, but I forget words when I
want to say something.
Friend: That's very common. Just keep practicing! What are you doing this weekend?
You: I'm going to visit a friend. We're going to have lunch and maybe watch a movie.
Friend: That sounds fun! Where does your friend live?
You: My friend lives in the city center.
📌 KEY GRAMMAR PATTERNS
Present Perfect: "I've been..."
Use this when talking about recent activities that continue to now.
Structure: I've been + verb-ing
Examples:
"I've been studying English every day."
"I've been working on a project."
"I've been feeling tired lately."
Future Plans: "I'm going to..."
Use this to talk about future plans.
Structure: I'm going to + verb
Examples:
"I'm going to visit my friend this weekend."
"I'm going to watch a movie tonight."
"I'm going to practice English every day."

💬 USEFUL EXPRESSIONS
10 Daily English Phrases You Must Know
1️⃣ "How are you today?"
Meaning: A common greeting to ask about someone's well-being
When to Use: When greeting friends, colleagues, or people you meet
Example:
"Hi Sarah! How are you today?"
Response: "I'm doing well, thank you!"
2️⃣ "Long time no see!"
Meaning: Used when you haven't seen someone for a while
When to Use: Greeting an old friend or someone you haven't seen recently
Example:
"Hey John! Long time no see! How have you been?"
Response: "I've been great! How about you?"
3️⃣ "I'd like to..."
Meaning: Polite way to express what you want
When to Use: Ordering food, making requests, expressing preferences
Examples:
"I'd like to order a coffee, please."
"I'd like to book a table for two."
"I'd like to ask you a question."
4️⃣ "Can I have...?"
Meaning: Polite way to ask for something
When to Use: Restaurants, shops, asking for information
Examples:
"Can I have the menu, please?"
"Can I have a glass of water?"
"Can I have your email address?"
5️⃣ "Would you like to...?"
Meaning: Polite way to invite someone or make a suggestion
When to Use: Making plans, inviting friends, offering something
Examples:
"Would you like to join us for dinner?"
"Would you like to go to the movies?"
"Would you like some coffee?"
6️⃣ "That sounds fun!"
Meaning: Showing enthusiasm about an idea or plan
When to Use: Responding positively to someone's suggestion
Example:
Friend: "We're going to the beach this weekend."
You: "That sounds fun! Can I join?"
7️⃣ "Don't worry about that."
Meaning: Reassuring someone; telling them not to stress
When to Use: Comforting someone who made a mistake or feels nervous
Example:
Student: "I'm sorry, I made a mistake."
Teacher: "Don't worry about that. Mistakes help you learn!"
8️⃣ "To be honest..."
Meaning: Introducing your honest thoughts or feelings
When to Use: When you want to share your true opinion
Examples:
"To be honest, I'm feeling a little nervous."
"To be honest, I don't really like that movie."
9️⃣ "See you around!"
Meaning: Casual goodbye (expecting to meet again soon)
When to Use: Saying goodbye to classmates, colleagues, neighbors
Example:
"It was nice talking to you! See you around!"
Response: "Yeah, see you!"
🔟 "How about you?"
Meaning: Asking the same question back to someone
When to Use: After answering a question, to show interest in the other person
Example:
Friend: "How are you?"
You: "I'm doing well, thank you. How about you?"
📖 GRAMMAR FOCUS
Key Grammar Patterns from This Lesson
1️⃣ PRESENT PERFECT: "I've been..."
When to Use:
Talk about recent activities that started in the past and continue until now.
Structure:
I've been + verb-ing
Examples from the Lesson:
"I've been busy with my studies."
"I've been studying English every day."
More Examples:
"I've been working on this project for two weeks."
"I've been feeling tired lately."
"I've been learning English for six months."
Practice:
Complete these sentences:

I've been __________ (practice) speaking with my friends.
I've been __________ (work) hard this week.
2️⃣ FUTURE PLANS: "I'm going to..."
When to Use:
Talk about future plans or intentions.
Structure:
I'm going to + base verb
Examples from the Lesson:
"I'm going to visit my friend this weekend."
"We're going to have lunch together."
More Examples:
"I'm going to study English tonight."
"She's going to start a new job next month."
"They're going to travel to Japan next year."
Practice:
Write your plans:
This weekend, I'm going to _________________.
Next month, I'm going to _________________.
Tomorrow, I'm going to _________________.
3️⃣ POLITE REQUESTS: "I'd like..." / "Can I have...?"
When to Use:
Making polite requests in restaurants, shops, or formal situations.
Structure:
I'd like + noun
Can I have + noun?
Examples from the Lesson:
"I'd like to order a chicken sandwich."
"Can I have the menu, please?"
"I'd like some water."
Difference:
"I'd like" = more formal, very polite
"Can I have" = common, polite but casual
Practice:
Rewrite these sentences politely:
Give me water. → _______________________
I want coffee. → _______________________
Show me the menu. → _______________________
4️⃣ ASKING ABOUT SOMEONE: "What do you do?"
Meaning:
This question asks about someone's job or studies.
Common Responses:
"I'm a student."
"I'm a teacher."
"I work in marketing."
"I study engineering."
Practice:
How would you answer "What do you do?"
Your answer: ________________________________
5️⃣ SHOWING INTEREST: "How about you?"
When to Use:
After answering a question, ask the same question back.
Examples:
A: "How are you?"
B: "I'm good, thanks. How about you?"
A: "Where are you from?"
B: "I'm from Brazil. How about you?"
Why It's Important:
It makes conversations feel natural and shows you care about the other person.

📌 MAIN LESSON POINTS
✅ Stop translating—think in English
✅ Practice shadowing daily
✅ Use simple, clear sentences
✅ Don't fear mistakes—they help you learn
✅ Speak out loud every day
✅ Real conversations are more important than perfect grammar
🎯 FLUENCY TIPS
1. Consistency Beats Perfection
Practice 10 minutes daily rather than 2 hours once a week.
2. Speak to Yourself
Describe what you're doing in English throughout the day.
3. Repeat, Repeat, Repeat
Shadowing builds automatic speech patterns.
4. Use What You Learn
Apply new words and phrases in real conversations.
5. Be Patient
Fluency takes time. Trust the process.
⚠️ COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
❌ Translating word-by-word from your native language
✅ Think in English phrases
❌ Saying only "I'm fine" every time
✅ Vary your responses: "I'm doing well," "I'm great," "I'm okay"
❌ Waiting until your English is perfect before speaking
✅ Start speaking now—mistakes are part of learning
❌ Only studying grammar without practicing speaking
✅ Balance grammar with real conversation practice
❌ Giving up when you feel stuck
✅ Keep practicing daily—progress adds up!
🌟 MOTIVATION & TIPS FOR SUCCESS
Dear English Learner,
You are doing something incredibly brave.
Learning a new language is not easy. There will be days when you feel frustrated. Days when
you forget words. Days when you think, "I'll never be fluent."
But here's the truth:
Every single person who speaks English fluently today was once exactly where you are now. They
made mistakes. They felt nervous. They forgot words in the middle of sentences.
The only difference between them and you is that they didn't give up.
🔥 REMEMBER THESE TRUTHS
✅ Mistakes are not failures—they are proof you're trying.
Every mistake teaches you something. Native speakers make mistakes too! Don't let fear of
mistakes stop you from speaking.
✅ Progress is not always visible, but it's always happening.
You might not notice improvement every day, but if you practice consistently, you WILL
improve. Trust the process.
✅ Fluency is built through repetition, not perfection.
You don't need perfect grammar to communicate. You need practice, confidence, and consistency.
✅ Speaking English is a skill, not a talent.
Nobody is "naturally good" at languages. It's a skill you build through daily practice—just
like playing an instrument or cooking.
✅ Small daily actions create massive results.
10 minutes of practice today.
10 minutes tomorrow.
10 minutes the day after.
In 30 days, that's 5 hours of practice.
In 6 months, that's 30 hours.
In 1 year, that's 60 hours.
That's enough to transform your English.
