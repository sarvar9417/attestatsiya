SECTION 2: MAIN LESSON CONTENT
🎯 Part 1: Understanding the Learning Challenge
The Common Problem: Translation Mindset
Most English learners face the same challenge: mental translation. When someone says "How are
you?" your brain follows this process:
Hear English question
Translate to your native language
Think of answer in native language
Translate answer back to English
Speak in English
Result: Slow, unnatural speech with frequent pauses.
The Solution: Direct English Thinking
This lesson teaches you to eliminate the translation step:
Hear English question
Think in simple English
Respond in English immediately
Result: Fast, natural, confident communication.
How We Achieve This
Through three powerful methods:
✓ Shadowing Practice – Repeat after native speakers immediately
✓ Real Conversations – Listen to natural dialogue patterns
✓ Daily Application – Use what you learn the same day
🎯 Part 2: Mastering Past Tense in Daily Conversation
Why Past Tense Matters
You use past tense every single day when you talk about:
What you did yesterday
Where you went last week
What you ate for lunch
Who you met last month
The Most Common Mistake
Wrong: "Yesterday I study English. I watch video. I eat dinner."
Correct: "Yesterday I studied English. I watched a video. I ate dinner."

🎯 Part 3: Building Natural Conversation Flow
Technique: Speaking From Feelings, Not Translation
The Secret Revealed:
When you talk about topics you love (family, hobbies, happy memories), your English flows more
naturally because you're expressing emotions, not translating words.
Example from the Lesson
Topic: Talking about a restaurant experience
Natural Flow:
"Last month, I went to a new restaurant with my brother. The food was delicious. I ordered
pasta, and my brother ordered pizza. We talked a lot, and we laughed. It was a very good day."
Why This Works:
The speaker is remembering a happy memory
Emotions reduce the need to translate
Natural vocabulary emerges from the experience
The story flows smoothly
💡 Practice Tip
Choose topics you feel passionate about. Your English will sound more natural because you're
focused on meaning, not grammar.

🎯 Part 6: Five Powerful Speaking Tips
Tip #1: Speak Every Day (Consistency is Key)
The Principle:
Even 5 minutes of daily speaking practice is more effective than 2 hours once a week.
How to Apply:
Talk to yourself in English while cooking
Describe what you see on your way to work
Repeat sentences from videos
Practice with a language partner online
Fizu's Experience:
"Now I do! I practice with videos like this every day."
Your Challenge:
Commit to speaking English for at least 5 minutes every single day this week.
Tip #2: Don't Be Afraid of Mistakes
The Truth About Mistakes:
Mistakes are not failures. Mistakes are feedback from your brain telling you what to learn
next.
Native Speakers Make Mistakes Too:
They forget words
They use wrong grammar sometimes
They mispronounce things
But they keep speaking!
Masu's Encouragement:
"Real people respect learners. If someone laughs, they don't matter. Focus on people who
support you."
Mindset Shift:
❌ Old Thinking: "I made a mistake. I'm bad at English."
✅ New Thinking: "I made a mistake. Now I know what to practice!"
Tip #3: Think in English, Don't Translate
The Problem with Translation:
Your brain works like this:
English → Your Language → Think → Your Language → English
The Solution:
Train your brain to work like this:
English → Think in English → Speak in English
How to Practice:
Name objects in English as you see them
"That's a cup."
"I see a red car."
Think simple thoughts in English
"I'm hungry."
"Today is beautiful."
Don't search for perfect words
Instead of "automobile," say "car"
Instead of "purchase," say "buy"
Progressive Practice:
Week 1: Think 5 English thoughts per day
Week 2: Think 10 English thoughts per day
Week 3: Try to think in English for 5 minutes
Week 4: Narrate your activities in English mentally
Tip #4: Use What You Learn Immediately
The Memory Principle:
You remember what you use, not what you read.
The 24-Hour Rule:
When you learn a new word or phrase, use it within 24 hours or your brain will forget it.
How to Apply Today's Lesson:

✓ Order food in English (even if you're alone, practice aloud)

Tip #5: Be Patient With Yourself
The Reality of Language Learning:
Some days you'll feel like a genius
Some days you'll forget basic words
Some days you'll speak fluently
Some days you'll struggle
All of this is NORMAL.
The Growth Mindset:
Learning English is not a straight line going up. It looks like this:
textProgress ↑ /\ /\ /\
 / \/ \ / \
Time → ________________
You have good days and challenging days, but the overall trend is always upward.
Masu's Promise:

Patience Practice:
Instead of asking "Why can't I speak perfectly yet?"
Ask "Am I better than I was last week?"

Expression 2: "How have you been?"
Meaning: What have you been doing? How is your life?
When to Use: When you want to know about someone's recent life
Grammar Note: Present Perfect tense (recent past connecting to now)
Example Response:
"I've been good, thanks!"
"I've been busy with work."
"I've been learning English!"
Similar Expressions:
"What have you been up to?"
"How's life?"
"How's everything?"
Expression 3: "Can I have...?"
Meaning: Polite way to ask for something
When to Use: Restaurants, shops, asking for items
Formula: Can I have + [noun] + please?
Examples:
"Can I have a coffee, please?"
"Can I have the menu, please?"
"Can I have your name, please?"
Alternative (more formal):
"May I have...?"
"Could I have...?"
Alternative (less formal):
"I'll have..."
"Give me..." (can sound rude—use carefully!)
Expression 4: "I'd like..."
Meaning: I want (polite form)
Full Form: I would like
When to Use: Restaurants, formal requests
Formula: I'd like + [noun] / I'd like to + [verb]
Examples:
"I'd like a glass of water, please."
"I'd like to order now."
"I'd like to ask a question."
Comparison:
Too direct: "I want coffee."
Polite: "I'd like coffee, please."
Very polite: "I'd like a coffee, please, if possible."
Expression 5: "I really appreciate it."
Meaning: Thank you very much; I'm very grateful
When to Use: When someone helps you or does something nice
Tone: Sincere, warm, thankful

Someone opens the door for you
Variations:
"I appreciate it."
"I really appreciate that."
"I appreciate your help."

"No problem!"
"You're welcome!"
"Anytime!"
Expression 6: "Would you like...?"
Meaning: Polite offer or question
When to Use: Offering something to someone
Formula: Would you like + [noun]?
Examples:
"Would you like fries with that?"
"Would you like some coffee?"
"Would you like to try it on?"
Answering:
Yes: "Yes, please." / "That sounds great!"

Expression 7: "Excuse me..."
Meaning: Polite way to get someone's attention or ask for help
When to Use:
Getting someone's attention

Spelling Rules:
Most verbs: Just add -ed
want → wanted
need → needed
Verbs ending in -e: Just add -d

Verbs ending in consonant + y: Change y to i, add -ed

Grammar Point #3: Using "Can I..." and "I'd like..." for Polite Requests
Structure 1: Can I have...?
Formula: Can I have + [noun] + please?

Usage: Stating what you want politely

⚡ Fluency Tips Quick Reference
DO:
✅ Speak English every day (even 5 minutes)
✅ Practice out loud, not just in your head
✅ Use simple words instead of searching for perfect words
✅ Record yourself and listen
✅ Repeat sentences from videos (shadowing)
✅ Think in English throughout the day
✅ Talk to yourself in English
✅ Make mistakes and learn from them
DON'T:
❌ Wait until you're "ready" to speak
❌ Translate every word in your head
❌ Fear making mistakes
❌ Study only grammar without practicing speaking
❌ Compare yourself to native speakers
❌ Give up when progress feels slow
❌ Study for hours once a week instead of daily
🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid
Mistake #1: Using Present Tense for Past Actions
❌ Wrong: Yesterday I go to the store.
✅ Correct: Yesterday I went to the store.
Mistake #2: Forgetting "Please" in Requests
❌ Rude: Give me water.
✅ Polite: Can I have water, please?
Mistake #3: Not Responding to "How are you?"
❌ Incomplete: (Just "Good")
✅ Complete: "I'm good, thanks! How are you?"
Mistake #4: Translating Word-by-Word
❌ Translation thinking: "I... have... hunger."
✅ English thinking: "I'm hungry."
Mistake #5: Being Too Afraid to Speak
❌ Waiting to be perfect: Never speaking
✅ Practicing actively: Speaking despite mistakes
